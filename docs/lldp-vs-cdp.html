PROTOCOLS / FIELD GUIDE

LLDP vs CDP: two ways a neighbor can identify itself

Both protocols let a network device advertise information to directly connected neighbors. Observing an advertisement can help identify the neighboring device and its advertised port without logging in to the switch.

LLDP

Link Layer Discovery Protocol is an IEEE 802.1AB standard designed for vendor-neutral neighbor discovery.

CDP

Cisco Discovery Protocol is a Cisco-developed protocol commonly found in Cisco environments.

What may be advertised

  • System, chassis, or device identity
  • Port identity and description
  • Platform or software description
  • Management address information

Fields are optional and implementation-dependent. A device may advertise only some of them.

The Port ID nuance

A Port ID is information advertised by the neighboring device. It may be useful evidence for finding a switch interface, but it is not a guaranteed physical-port mapping. Record it as the advertised Port ID and corroborate it when the ticket requires certainty.

Why an advertisement may not appear

  • The protocol may be disabled globally or on the interface.
  • The adapter, driver, dock, or virtual networking layer may not expose the frame.
  • The observation window may not include an advertisement interval.
  • The selected adapter may not be the one connected to the jack.

ABSENCE IS NOT A CONNECTIVITY VERDICT

No LLDP/CDP advertisement observed does not prove that the link, switch, VLAN, gateway, DNS, or internet path failed.

How LANVEXA fits

LANVEXA is designed to observe these passive advertisements as one part of a broader first-pass workflow alongside basic connectivity checks. Exact capture and parsing behavior remains subject to desktop-application verification.
